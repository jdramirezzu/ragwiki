Vector search or WikiLLM?

Build the one your documents need, or both and let your questions decide.

Vector search (RAG) finds the closest paragraphs every time you ask. A WikiLLM has a model write a linked wiki once, when you add files, and then reads it. This lab builds either one from your files, answers with it, and compares both with the same model.

    How they differ

    From a study with 100 scientific papers, 100 questions and the same model for both.

    When the work happensAt every questionOnce, when a file is added
    BuildingFree with local embeddings; minutes2 model calls per file; 1.6–2.9 USD and 1.8 h for 100 papers
    Cost per question1×7.2× and 4.1× the time

    Questions that join several documentsMisses what it does not retrieveStrongest: pages already join sources
    When files changeRe-embed the changed filePages are rewritten; old values may linger
    What you get besides answersNothing to readA wiki of linked pages you can browse
    Typical failure“Not in your files” when retrieval missesAnswers when it should abstain; misses what it did not capture

    Choose vector search when

    • budget or speed matter most
    • files change often
    • questions ask for one fact from one document
    • you will ask few questions

    Choose a WikiLLM when

    • questions join or compare several documents
    • files are stable and you will ask many questions
    • you also want to read and browse the knowledge
    • a better answer is worth several times the cost

    How to read these results
    • Answered counts answers with at least one valid citation. It is not correctness: expected answers and your verdicts measure quality.
    • The WikiLLM pays once to write its pages and then spends more per question, because it reads them. Vector search has no model cost to build and makes one call per question.
    • In the study behind wikinotes the WikiLLM answered 0.70 of the answerable questions and RAG 0.48 with 5 retrieved paragraphs, at 7.2× the cost per question. With 20 paragraphs the gap fell to +0.06 and was no longer significant. Try more than one value of k.
